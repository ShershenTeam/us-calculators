## How to calculate how much paint you need

1. **Add up the wall area.** For a room: 2 × (length + width) × wall height. A 12 × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls: 2 × 24 × 8 = 384 sq ft.
2. **Subtract doors and windows.** One 20 sq ft door and one 12 sq ft window: 384 − 32 = **352 sq ft**.
3. **Multiply by the number of coats.** Two coats: 704 sq ft.
4. **Divide by the coverage on the can.** At 350 sq ft per gallon: 704 ÷ 350 = **2.01 gallons**.
5. **Round up to what the store sells.** 2.01 gallons is a little over 8 quarts, so buy **2 gallons and 1 quart**.

The calculator does this for every room at once. Add rooms with *Add another room or wall*; use *One wall* for an accent wall or *Known area* for a fence or a surface you have already measured. Open *How it's calculated* to see every room's numbers.

## How far a gallon goes

Coverage is printed on the can and on the manufacturer's data sheet. For interior wall paint it is typically:

| Paint | Coverage per gallon, per coat | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Sherwin-Williams ProMar 200 Zero VOC Interior Latex | 350–400 sq ft | Product data sheet 101.81A |
| Benjamin Moore Regal Select Interior | 400–450 sq ft | Technical data sheet N548 |

The calculator starts at **350 sq ft per gallon**, the low end, so you are more likely to finish with a little left over than to run out in the middle of a wall. If your can says more, choose 400 or 450 or type the exact figure. Rough plaster, textured walls and bare drywall soak up more paint than smooth painted walls.

## Coats and primer

- **Two coats** is the default. Benjamin Moore recommends two coats for full color development; Sherwin-Williams specifies two coats of finish paint in its systems for drywall, wood, plaster and masonry.
- **Primer** goes on new drywall and bare wood: Sherwin-Williams lists one coat of primer under two coats of paint for new drywall. A coat of primer also helps when you change to a much darker or lighter color. Choose *+ 1 coat primer*; it is shown on its own line, with its own coverage.
- **One coat** can work when you repaint the same color over clean, sound paint.

## Walls and ceiling: two different paints

Ceilings are usually painted with a flat ceiling paint, and walls with an eggshell or satin wall paint, so the calculator gives two separate totals. Choose *+ Ceiling* on a room to include it. The ceiling area is simply length × width.

## Buying: gallons, quarts and 5-gallon buckets

Paint is sold in quarts (¼ gallon), gallons and 5-gallon buckets. The calculator rounds up to whole quarts and then packs the order: 5-gallon buckets first, then gallons, then quarts — and when you would need three quarts, it buys a gallon instead. Keep a little of each color for touch-ups, and mix all the cans of one color in a larger bucket before you start ("boxing") so the shade is even across walls.
