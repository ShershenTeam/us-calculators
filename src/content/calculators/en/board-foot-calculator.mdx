## How to calculate board feet

A **board foot** is a piece of wood 1 foot wide, 1 foot long and 1 inch thick — 144 cubic inches. For any board:

**board feet = thickness (in) × width (in) × length (ft) ÷ 12**

**Worked example.** Ten 2×4s, 8 ft long, and four 2×6s, 12 ft long:

- 2×4 × 8 ft: 2 × 4 × 8 ÷ 12 = 5.33 bd ft each × 10 = **53.33 bd ft**
- 2×6 × 12 ft: 2 × 6 × 12 ÷ 12 = 12 bd ft each × 4 = **48 bd ft**
- Total: **101.33 board feet**, 14 pieces, 128 linear feet

That is the calculator's starting list. Change it to your own: pick a nominal size, set the length and the number of pieces, and add a line for each size you are buying.

## Nominal or actual size?

Lumber is named by its **nominal** size, which is larger than the wood you can measure. The American Softwood Lumber Standard (PS 20, published by NIST) sets the dressed sizes — and also says that **board feet are counted with the nominal size**: "multiplying the nominal thickness in inches … by the nominal width in feet by the length in feet."

| Nominal | Actual (dry, dressed) | Board feet per 8 ft | per 12 ft | per 16 ft |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1×4 | ¾ × 3½ in | 2.67 | 4.00 | 5.33 |
| 1×6 | ¾ × 5½ in | 4.00 | 6.00 | 8.00 |
| 1×8 | ¾ × 7¼ in | 5.33 | 8.00 | 10.67 |
| 2×4 | 1½ × 3½ in | 5.33 | 8.00 | 10.67 |
| 2×6 | 1½ × 5½ in | 8.00 | 12.00 | 16.00 |
| 2×8 | 1½ × 7¼ in | 10.67 | 16.00 | 21.33 |
| 2×10 | 1½ × 9¼ in | 13.33 | 20.00 | 26.67 |
| 2×12 | 1½ × 11¼ in | 16.00 | 24.00 | 32.00 |
| 4×4 | 3½ × 3½ in | 10.67 | 16.00 | 21.33 |

So use the nominal size when you compare prices or check an invoice; use the actual size when you design — a wall of 2×4 studs is 3½ inches thick, not 4.

## Hardwood: thickness in quarters

Hardwood dealers sell rough lumber by board feet, with the thickness in **quarters of an inch**:

| Thickness | Inches | Board feet in a 6 in × 8 ft board |
|---|---|---|
| 4/4 | 1 | 4.00 |
| 5/4 | 1¼ | 5.00 |
| 6/4 | 1½ | 6.00 |
| 8/4 | 2 | 8.00 |

Hardwood boards come in random widths, so measure each board's width (or the total width of a stack of the same length) and enter it with the thickness chosen from the list. Rough boards lose some thickness when you plane them; the board feet you pay for are counted on the rough size.

## Pricing: per piece, per board foot or per linear foot

Home centers price construction lumber **per piece**; lumberyards often quote **per board foot** or per thousand board feet (MBF); trim and molding are sold **per linear foot**. Each line in the calculator can use its own basis, so you can compare a lumberyard quote with store prices for the same list. To convert, a price per board foot × board feet per piece = price per piece.

## Extra for waste and defects

Some boards will have knots, splits or wane where you need a clean cut, and offcuts are rarely long enough to reuse. Add a percentage under *Extra for waste & defects* to raise both the board feet and the cost. How much depends on the grade and the project — a small allowance for construction framing, more for furniture where you cut around defects.
