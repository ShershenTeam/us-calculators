## How to calculate square footage

For a rectangular room, **multiply the length by the width**. Both measurements must be in feet; the result is in square feet.

1. **Measure the length** of the room wall to wall, at floor level, in feet and inches.
2. **Measure the width** the same way.
3. **Convert inches to feet** — divide by 12 (6 in = 0.5 ft) — or simply type `12'6"` into the calculator.
4. **Multiply**: 12.5 ft × 10 ft = 125 sq ft.

To measure a whole floor, repeat this for every room and add the results. The calculator does the adding for you: tap *Add another room*, give each one a name, and the total updates as you type. The room schedule next to the result lists every room, which is what you hand to a flooring store or contractor.

### Area formulas for other shapes

| Shape | Formula | Example | Area |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rectangle or square | length × width | 14 × 12 ft | 168 sq ft |
| L-shaped room | (L₁ × W₁) + (L₂ × W₂) | 20 × 12 + 8 × 6 ft | 288 sq ft |
| Circle | π × (diameter ÷ 2)² | 10 ft across | 78.54 sq ft |
| Triangle | base × height ÷ 2 | 10 ft base, 8 ft high | 40 sq ft |
| Trapezoid | (side a + side b) ÷ 2 × height | 10 and 14 ft sides, 8 ft apart | 96 sq ft |

The height of a triangle or trapezoid is measured at a right angle to the base, not along a slanted wall.

## Rooms that are not rectangles

Very few rooms are perfect rectangles. The method is always the same: **break the floor into simple shapes, find each area, then add or subtract**.

- **L-shaped and T-shaped rooms** — two or three rectangles that do not overlap. Choose *L-shape* or add each part as a separate room.
- **Bay windows** — a trapezoid on top of the main rectangle.
- **Rounded alcoves** — half a circle: π × radius² ÷ 2. A 6 ft-wide alcove is 3.14 × 3² ÷ 2 = 14.1 sq ft; add it as a *Known area*.
- **Things you will not cover** — a kitchen island, a fireplace hearth, a built-in tub or a stairwell opening. Tap *Subtract a cut-out*: the piece is drawn in ochre and its area comes off the total.

Closets are usually included when you buy flooring for a bedroom, because the floor runs into them. Add a closet as its own small room so you can see it on the schedule.

## Square footage of a whole house: what counts

The number in a home listing is not the same as the sum of your room measurements. Since 2022, Fannie Mae has required appraisers to measure homes using **ANSI Z765-2021**, the American National Standard for single-family square footage. The main rules:

- **Exterior dimensions.** The house is measured around the outside walls, so wall thickness is included. That is why listed square footage is larger than the floor you would carpet.
- **Finished area only.** The space must be suitable for year-round use, with walls, floors and ceilings finished like the rest of the house, and reachable directly from other finished space.
- **Ceiling height of at least 7 ft.** Under a sloped ceiling, at least half of the room must have 7 ft of headroom, and no part with less than 5 ft counts.
- **Above and below grade are separate.** A finished basement is reported on its own line, not added to the above-grade total.
- **Not included:** garages, unfinished basements and attics, open porches and decks, and openings in an upper floor such as a two-story foyer.

If you are checking a listing or an appraisal, measure the outside of the house. If you are buying materials, measure inside each room — that is what this calculator is set up for.

## How much extra to buy for waste

You will always cut some pieces to fit walls, doorways and corners, and some boards or tiles arrive damaged. Order more than the measured area:

| How it is installed | Extra to add | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Planks or tile laid straight | **5 %** | Cuts only at the walls |
| Tile, vinyl, most patterns | **10 %** | More cuts; spares from the same dye lot |
| Diagonal, herringbone, many angles | **15 %** | Every edge piece is cut at an angle |

Mullican Flooring recommends adding 5–7 % for hardwood laid straight and 10–15 % on a diagonal; Daltile recommends buying about 10 % more tile than you measured. Lower-grade boards with more knots and color variation may need extra on top, because the installer sets aside pieces you do not want on the floor.

Choose the matching preset under *Extra for waste*, or type your own percentage.

## How many boxes of flooring you need

Flooring is sold by the box, and every box lists how many square feet it covers — often between 18 and 30 sq ft for laminate and vinyl plank. To find the number of boxes:

**boxes = (square footage × (1 + waste %)) ÷ coverage per box, rounded up**

For a 14 × 12 ft room with 10 % waste and boxes of 22.69 sq ft: 168 × 1.10 = 184.8 sq ft, and 184.8 ÷ 22.69 = 8.14, so you buy **9 boxes**. Always round up — stores will not sell a fraction of a box. Open *Boxes and price* in the calculator, type the coverage from the box label, and the count appears with the result.

Keep one unopened box after the job. If a board is damaged years later, a match from the same production run is hard to find.

## Converting square feet to yards, meters and acres

| From | To | Multiply by | Exact? |
|---|---|---|---|
| square feet | square meters | 0.09290304 | yes — 1 ft = 0.3048 m exactly |
| square meters | square feet | 10.7639104 | rounded |
| square feet | square yards | ÷ 9 | yes — 1 yd = 3 ft |
| square feet | acres | ÷ 43,560 | yes |
| acres | square feet | 43,560 | yes |

Carpet is often priced per square yard: divide square feet by 9 and compare. A 168 sq ft room is 18.67 sq yd. The calculator shows the result in square feet, square meters, square yards and acres at the same time, and you can enter a price per square foot or per square yard.
