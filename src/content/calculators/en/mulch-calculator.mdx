## How to calculate how much mulch you need

1. **Measure each bed** — length × width for a rectangle; for a round bed, π × (diameter ÷ 2)².
2. **Choose the depth** — 2–3 inches for most beds.
3. **Multiply area × depth ÷ 12** to get cubic feet.
4. **Divide by the bag size** for bags, or **by 27** for cubic yards.

**Worked example.** A front bed 20 ft × 4 ft, 3 inches deep:

- Area: 20 × 4 = 80 sq ft
- Volume: 80 × 3 ÷ 12 = 20 cu ft = 20 ÷ 27 = **0.74 yd³**
- Bags: 20 ÷ 2 = **10 bags** of 2 cu ft, or 7 bags of 3 cu ft

Add every bed and tree as its own area: the calculator keeps each one's depth, adds them up and shows the step-by-step working. For a bed with an odd outline, the <Calc slug="square-footage-calculator">square footage calculator</Calc> can find its area first.

One cubic yard of bulk mulch covers 162 sq ft at 2 inches, 108 sq ft at 3 inches and 81 sq ft at 4 inches. Metric bags are sold in liters: a 2 cu ft bag is about 56 L, a 3 cu ft bag about 85 L.

## How deep to spread mulch

| Guidance | Depth | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Around trees | 2–3 in, in a circle at least 3 ft across | University of Illinois Extension |
| Trees and shrubs | 1–3 in | University of Maryland Extension |
| Coarse arborist wood chips | 3–4 in, never more than 4 in | Oregon State University Extension |
| Bark mulch | no more than 3 in | Oregon State University Extension |

Fine, shredded mulch packs down and needs less depth; coarse chips stay open and need a little more to block light. The *Depth for all beds* buttons set 2, 3 or 4 inches everywhere at once, and the calculator warns above 4 inches.

## Mulch around trees: rings, not volcanoes

All three extension services say the same thing: **keep mulch off the trunk**. Maryland advises about 3 inches of clearance, Illinois at least 4; mulch piled against the bark keeps it wet and invites disease, and Oregon State notes it shelters voles that chew the bark.

That is why the calculator has a **Tree ring** shape. Enter the outer diameter of the ring — at least 3 feet — and the bare circle around the trunk, and only the ring itself is counted. A 6 ft ring with a 1 ft bare circle, 3 inches deep, takes 6.9 cu ft: about 3½ bags of 2 cu ft per tree. Put the number of similar trees under *Identical trees*.

## Topping up old mulch

Organic mulch decomposes and has to be replenished as it breaks down, as Illinois Extension puts it. You do not need a full new layer, only the difference. Measure what is left (poke a ruler into the bed), enter it under **Mulch already there**, and the calculator orders only what is missing: 1 inch left with a 3 inch target means adding 2 inches, a third less mulch than starting over.

## Bags or bulk

Bags are tidy, easy to carry and right for a few beds. Bulk mulch is delivered by the cubic yard — the calculator rounds your order up to the next half yard, the usual delivery step — and usually costs less per yard once you need more than one or two yards. Put a bag price or a bulk price per yard under *Price* to compare them.
